In Memory Of

Peter Cullen

July 28, 1941  –  August 2026

The voice that told a generation: "Till all are one."

A Voice That Led

Peter Claver Cullen was a Canadian voice actor whose career began in Montreal radio and stretched across more than sixty years of animation, film, and television. He voiced Ironhide, the mournful Eeyore in the Winnie the Pooh franchise, Monterey Jack in Chip 'n Dale: Rescue Rangers, and the guttural clicks of the Predator — but it was one role, first recorded in a Burbank casting office in 1983, that became his legacy.

Becoming Optimus Prime

When Cullen read the character breakdown for a truck that transformed into a heroic robot, he thought of his older brother Larry, a Marine captain whose quiet strength after returning from Vietnam shaped the calm, restrained authority Cullen brought to the role. That instinct — power held in check by compassion — became the defining sound of Optimus Prime, first heard in the original 1984 Transformers cartoon and carried by Cullen through the 2007 live-action films, video games, and television revivals for the next four decades.

A Legacy in Motion

  1. 1984 Voices Optimus Prime for the first time in The Transformers
  2. 1986 Optimus Prime's death in The Transformers: The Movie sparks nationwide fan outcry
  3. 2007 Returns to the role for Michael Bay's live-action Transformers
  4. 2010 Inducted into the Transformers Hall of Fame at BotCon
  5. 2023 Receives a Lifetime Achievement Award at the Children's and Family Emmy Awards
  6. 2026 Passes away peacefully at home, surrounded by family

His family asked that his legacy be honored not with sorrow alone, but with compassion, integrity, and loyalty — the same values he gave a voice to for forty years. Autobots and admirers alike, rolling out one last time in his memory.
